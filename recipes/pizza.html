Pizza

Description

Pizza is a beloved savory dish consisting of a round, flattened base of leavened wheat-based dough topped with tomatoes, cheese, and various other ingredients, baked at a high temperature. While flatbreads with toppings have existed in the Mediterranean for centuries, modern pizza originated in Naples, Italy, in the 18th and 19th centuries as an affordable, quick meal for the working class, before spreading worldwide and evolving into countless regional styles.

Ingredients

  • For the pizza dough:
    • All-purpose flour (or bread flour for a chewier crust)
    • Warm water
    • Active dry yeast (or instant yeast)
    • Olive oil
    • Salt
    • Sugar (a pinch, to help activate the yeast)
  • For the sauce:
    • Tomato puree or crushed tomatoes
    • Garlic (minced or powdered)
    • Dried oregano
    • Olive oil
    • Salt and black pepper
  • For the toppings:
    • Mozzarella cheese (shredded or sliced)
    • Dried oregano (to sprinkle on top)
    • Green olives (optional, classic Argentine style)
    • Fresh basil leaves (optional)

Steps

  1. Activate the yeast: Pour the warm water into a small bowl or cup. Add the yeast and a pinch of sugar, stir gently, and let it sit for about 5 to 10 minutes until it becomes foamy and bubbly on top. (Note: If using instant yeast, you can skip this step and mix it directly into the flour).
  2. Mix the dry ingredients: In a large mixing bowl, combine the flour and salt, mixing them well so the salt doesn't come into direct, concentrated contact with the yeast later. Make a well in the center of the flour.
  3. Form the dough: Pour the foamy yeast mixture and the olive oil into the center of the flour well. Mix with a wooden spoon or your hands until a rough, shaggy dough forms and no dry flour remains at the bottom of the bowl.
  4. Knead the dough: Transfer the dough onto a lightly floured surface. Knead it with the heels of your hands for about 8 to 10 minutes—pushing it forward, folding it over, and turning it—until the dough becomes smooth, elastic, and no longer sticky.
  5. Let the dough rise (First Proof): Lightly grease a clean bowl with olive oil, place the ball of dough inside, and turn it to coat it with oil. Cover the bowl with a clean kitchen towel or plastic wrap and let it rise in a warm, draft-free spot for 1 to 1.5 hours, or until it doubles in size.
  6. Prepare the tomato sauce: While the dough rises, mix the crushed tomatoes or tomato puree in a bowl with minced garlic, olive oil, dried oregano, salt, and black pepper. You can use this sauce uncooked for a fresh taste, or simmer it over low heat for 10 minutes for a thicker consistency.
  7. Shape the pizza base: Punch down the risen dough gently to release the air. Transfer it to a lightly oiled pizza pan. Using your fingertips, press and stretch the dough outward from the center toward the edges until it covers the pan, leaving a slightly thicker rim around the border for the crust. (Optional: Let the stretched dough rest in the pan for 15 minutes for a fluffier, Argentine-style "pizza al molde").
  8. Par-bake the crust (Optional but recommended): Spread a thin layer of tomato sauce over the dough and bake it on the bottom rack of the hot oven for about 5 to 7 minutes until the crust starts to firm up and lightly brown. This prevents a soggy center.
  9. Add the cheese and toppings: Remove the pan from the oven, add a little more tomato sauce if desired, and cover generously with shredded or sliced mozzarella cheese. Sprinkle dried oregano on top and add green olives or your favorite toppings.
  10. Bake and serve: Return the pizza to the middle or upper rack of the oven for another 8 to 12 minutes, until the crust is golden brown and the cheese is completely melted, bubbling, and slightly browned in spots. Remove from the oven, let it cool for 2 minutes, slice, and serve hot.
